## Autofocus

This is one of the major advantages of Camera Module 3.

**PDAF = Phase Detection Autofocus**

The camera has:

* Phase-detection autofocus
* Motorised lens actuator
* Software-controlled focus
* Continuous/automatic focusing capability

This is particularly useful for your drone because the distance to the target changes during the approach.

```text
15 m
 ↓
Detection
 ↓
10 m
 ↓
5 m
 ↓
2 m
 ↓
Target
```

The camera can adjust focus rather than relying on a fixed-focus lens. ([Raspberry Pi Docs][1])

# IR-CUT system

This is the major special feature.

The module has an onboard **IR-CUT dual-bandpass filter** that can be switched according to the application. ([WaveShare][3])

### Day

```text
Visible light
      ↓
   IR-CUT ON
      ↓
 Normal colour image
```

### Night + IR illuminator

```text
IR illumination
      ↓
   IR-CUT OFF
      ↓
 Infrared image
```

Waveshare notes that an external IR light source is required for night vision. ([Waveshare][4])

# 4. The biggest differences

### Camera Module 3

**IMX708**

```text
11.9 MP
4608×2592
75° FoV
f/1.8
PDAF
AUTOFOCUS
HDR
```

### IMX477 B

**IMX477**

```text
12.3 MP
4056×3040
113° FoV
f/2.8
FIXED FOCUS
IR-CUT
```

So don't let **12.3 MP vs 11.9 MP** make you think the IMX477 automatically has higher useful resolution.

For your application, **optics and pixels-on-target matter much more than the MP number**.

# 5. One correction from my previous answer

I want to correct one thing from the earlier comparison.

I previously treated the IMX477's **113° diagonal FoV** as though we could directly derive its horizontal/vertical FoV with a simple geometric conversion. That's not sufficiently precise without knowing the exact lens/sensor geometry and distortion model.

Waveshare officially specifies:

> **113° diagonal FoV**

and **< −1.5% distortion**.

So for engineering calculations, I'd use Waveshare's 113° diagonal figure and the actual calibrated camera intrinsics rather than assuming exact horizontal/vertical FoV. ([WaveShare][3])
